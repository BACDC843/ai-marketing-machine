# Your first week

Five days, about twenty minutes each. The order matters more than the pace — every day builds on the one before, and day one is the one that makes the rest work.

**Deliver one day at a time.** A five-day plan handed over in a single message is a five-day plan nobody starts.

---

## Day 1 — Get the brand right

Say **"set up my business profile"** and give it your website.

It reads the site, then asks you six things it can't get from a website. Answer them properly — this is the twenty minutes that decides whether everything afterwards sounds like you or sounds like marketing.

**The last question is the one that matters:** *what do you believe about your industry that a lot of people in it would disagree with?* Most people skip it. Don't. Content with a point of view gets shared; content that agrees with everyone gets scrolled past, and this answer is where that point of view comes from.

**Done when:** `doctor` reports your brand files as real rather than placeholder or starter-pack defaults.

---

## Day 2 — Make one post

Say **"make me a post."**

If you don't have a topic, say "you pick" — it'll suggest three angles drawn from your own brand and calendar.

Read what comes back and ask yourself one question: **does this sound like me?** Not "is it good" — does it sound like you. If it doesn't, that's day 1's job unfinished, and it's worth going back rather than pushing on. Every post after this inherits the same profile.

**Done when:** an image is saved in your `visuals/` folder and you'd be willing to publish the caption.

---

## Day 3 — Make three more, and see the difference

Say **"make me a post"** three more times, on three different topics.

The point isn't volume. It's calibration — after four posts you'll see which angles feel right and which don't, and that tells you more about your own brand than any amount of thinking about it will.

**Tell it when something's wrong.** "Too formal", "stop saying we're passionate", "that's not what we actually sell" — those corrections go back into the profile and every future post improves. This is the step most people skip and it's the one that compounds.

**Done when:** you have four pieces in `examples/` and you've corrected at least one thing about the voice.

---

## Day 4 — Plan instead of react

Say **"plan a week of content."**

You get seven posts, each graded and revised until it clears the bar, each with its own graphic. Not to publish all seven — to have a bank so you're never staring at a blank page on a Tuesday.

Then look at the spread. If five of the seven are the same kind of post, your content pillars need work — say so and ask for a rebalance.

**Done when:** you have a week of posts saved and you've read the calendar as a whole rather than post by post.

---

## Day 5 — Decide how this actually runs

Two decisions, and both are fine to answer "not yet".

**Publishing.** If you want posts scheduled rather than copy-pasted, connect Blotato. If you'd rather paste them yourself, that works permanently — nothing degrades.

**Measurement.** Connecting Meta lets real reach and engagement flow back into your calendar, so the system starts telling you which of *your* posts actually worked rather than which ones scored well before publishing. It's the hardest connection to set up and it's genuinely optional. Everything works without it.

Run **`doctor`** and read it once, properly. It'll tell you what's connected, what isn't, and what each gap costs you.

**Done when:** you know what you're connecting and what you're not, and why.

---

## What good looks like after a week

- Brand profile filled in from your own words, not a template
- Four to ten pieces in `examples/`
- At least one voice correction fed back in
- A week of content banked
- A clear decision on publishing and measurement

## The two things people get wrong

**Rushing day 1.** Every post inherits the profile. Twenty minutes here saves an argument with the output every day after; skipping it means fighting the same generic tone forever.

**Not pushing back.** The corrections are the mechanism, not a complaint. Saying "that's not how we talk" is how the profile gets better. People who accept the first draft every time get a system that never improves; people who argue with it get one that sounds like them within a fortnight.
